# Evidence-based scoped review

Identify the manuscript, requested unit and purpose, actual study design, available sources and whether revision is requested. A review request alone does not authorize editing the manuscript. A separate memo can be saved without changing it. Local review is useful even without a target venue or complete manuscript profile.

Use current workspace terminology and argument boundaries as author decisions to understand, not scientific truth to defend uncritically. Inspect the underlying sources when questioning an adopted claim. Report a conflict with evidence; propose a correction instead of overwriting confirmed records. Current explicit author corrections must be reconciled with older records rather than discarded because an archive looks authoritative.

Choose relevant dimensions: framing and contribution; design and assumptions; results and claim scope; interpretation and alternatives; displays; English expression; delivery requirements. These are lenses, not mandatory sequential passes. Method and domain modules deepen a relevant check without triggering other modules automatically. A keyword or citation alone does not establish the study design. Ask only when a material ambiguity cannot be resolved from the manuscript.

For a substantive claim, trace question, method, evidence, answer and bounded contribution. Look for unanswered questions, unsupported mechanisms, inconsistent comparison bases and assumptions left unstated. Distinguish absent reporting from a demonstrated design defect, unavailable evidence from proven omission, and expected results from lack of scientific value. A finding need not be surprising to be useful.

For each material issue give a location, specific problem, evidence or uncertainty, impact, and smallest useful repair or author decision. Keep these dimensions separate:

- Severity: critical threatens core validity or integrity; major materially affects inference or reproducibility; minor is localized reporting or comprehension; note is a nonblocking suggestion.
- Evidence: supported by the inspected artifact/source; tentative interpretation needing confirmation; unverified because evidence is unavailable.
- Classification: validity, reporting, venue requirement, preference, or prose.
- Coverage: checked, not applicable, outside scope, or unavailable. Checked can still have an open issue.

Do not assign severity by the ease of a language edit. A speculative severe concern remains tentative. A preference is normally nonblocking. Source-bound requirements need current provenance and applicability. Keep author instructions, evidence, scientific obligations and preferences distinguishable; surface conflicts.

Follow actual section functions and quote only short anchors. Do not invent comments for absent sections, target a comment quota, produce an unsupported quality score, or infer AI authorship. Preserve accurate text and no-change conclusions. Bibliographic identity, citation formatting and claim-level support are different checks.

The memo should state scope, artifacts and versions, selected resources with reasons, findings, coverage and unavailable sources, and next useful actions. Readiness is relative to the named purpose and actual checks. A saved report or clean pattern scan cannot establish scientific validity or submission readiness.

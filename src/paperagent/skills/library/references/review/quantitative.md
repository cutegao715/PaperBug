# Quantitative, measurement and SEM review

Use when measurement, scales, latent variables, SEM or statistical comparisons support substantive claims. A descriptive mean alone does not require a psychometrics audit.

Identify the estimand, observation unit, sampling frame and dependence structure before judging a statistical method. Inspect missingness, exclusions, pairing, clustering, repeated observations, weights, uncertainty and multiplicity as relevant. Unequal group sizes alone do not determine the correct test. A significant result in one group and a nonsignificant result in another do not establish a between-group difference.

For scales, inspect construct definitions, item sources and adaptations, scoring, translations, reverse coding, reliability and relevant validity evidence. Reliability alone does not establish validity or unidimensionality. Exploratory item removal differs from confirmatory evaluation and may change construct coverage.

For factor/SEM models, distinguish measurement and structural models; inspect identification, estimator assumptions, item scale, missing-data treatment, fit, loadings, residuals, theoretical modifications and alternatives. Common thresholds are context-dependent guidance, not universal gates. Group comparisons require measurement comparability appropriate to the claimed contrast, not every possible invariance test indiscriminately.

Check whether mediation or causal language exceeds timing and design. Trace coefficients, reference groups, signs, sample counts, uncertainty and subgroup labels to the correct analysis. Nondetection is not demonstrated equivalence; preserve equivalence claims supported by appropriate bounds and evidence rather than banning the word.

Describe the smallest missing analysis or reporting evidence needed for the particular claim. Do not reconstruct unknown statistics or demand new analyses merely because a checklist mentions them.

# Argument and reader function

Use this guide for substantive planning or restructuring. A grammar edit does not require a new outline. Follow the author's section order; a conventional article structure is an option, not an execution graph.

Trace the affected links in both directions:

- Gap or purpose → question → method → expected evidence.
- Actual evidence → finding → interpretation → bounded contribution.

Keep planned evidence separate from available evidence. In an early outline, mark a missing result as a need, not an invented finding. For an existing manuscript, reverse-outline its actual paragraph openings before proposing changes; do not pretend it already has the desired argument.

An extended outline can use proposed topic sentences followed by evidence pointers and unresolved needs. The outline should be readable as an argument. It need not have a fixed number of paragraphs or numbered research questions.

For an Introduction, identify the problem, the relevant knowledge, the specific unresolved question, why that gap matters, and what this study can contribute. Use the closest prior work to delimit the contribution. Do not claim “no study” or “the first” from an incomplete search. A reference being topically relevant does not establish its support for the gap.

Before drafting a substantive paragraph, identify its function, narrow claim, evidence, development and bridge. These are questions for planning, not five mandatory sentences. Read available preceding and following text; state when that context is unavailable. Group literature by its evidentiary role instead of reproducing discovery order. A connector cannot repair missing reasoning.

Map section functions to the genre. A theoretical paper may use premises and derivation; a review uses search, selection and synthesis; a methods paper uses design and evaluation. Never fabricate empirical Results to fill IMRAD headings.

For a revision, preserve the author's actual aim: comparing, estimating, explaining, predicting, validating and demonstrating are different claims. Changes to the research question or contribution can affect Methods, Results, Discussion, Abstract and title. Identify the affected dependencies; do not automatically rewrite every file.

# Discussion, limitations, conclusion and abstract

Read current Results and their evidence boundaries before summarizing. A prior Abstract is not the authoritative source for a new result.

In Discussion, separate direct findings, literature-based interpretation, alternative explanations, implications and speculation. Explain what each result adds at the level the design supports. If extension analyses exist, identify exactly what remained stable and what was not tested. Avoid turning model sensitivity into universal robustness.

For limitations, name a documented design, measurement, data or scope constraint and the inference it limits. Do not invent a direction of bias. Distinguish a genuine research boundary from a correctable error; repair an error when authorized instead of disguising it as a limitation. Future work does not substitute for stating the current boundary.

Conclusion should explain what the study did, what it established and what that adds within its scope. Preserve the main comparison and essential qualifiers. Do not introduce new evidence, replay every figure or force a grand policy recommendation.

For Abstract, supply the necessary information functions: problem/gap, purpose, method and its role, subject/scope, supported main finding, contribution/boundary. Combine these naturally or follow a verified structured format. They are not six fixed blocks or sentence quotas. No universal word limit applies.

When findings do not yet exist, produce a clearly provisional outline or purpose/method draft and identify the missing findings. Do not write a completed-results abstract for unfinished research. Distinguish a conference proposal abstract from an abstract reporting completed work according to the actual call.

Read the abstract independently. A reader should understand the subject, task, method, comparator, main answer and qualified contribution without internal abbreviations or figures. After compression, check whether a population, denominator, condition or limitation disappeared, or whether a descriptive comparison became validation or equivalence.

Substantive changes elsewhere may make summaries stale. Recheck affected claims against the revised manuscript; authors can still choose any writing order.
